Speaker Script — LED runs in SNOLAB Run 7

Slide 1: Title(about 15 s)

Hi everyone, I'm Zhiheng. Today I'll show the LED data from Run 7. Four points: who sees which LED, amplitude stability, trigger stability, and pulse templates.

Slide 2: Background(about 45 s)

First, the background. This is Run 7 data after the Cf calibration. The DAQ script runs in a loop: low-background data, then one LED run, about every 2 hours. Each line on the top is one LED run. From September 15 to 21 there are 47 of them, and we use 45.

Slide 3: One LED run(about 40 s)

Inside one LED run: in phase one, 15 LEDs flash. Then 3 seconds off. In phase two, 12 LEDs flash. Each LED flashes once per second, 151 times. Each trigger reads out only the detector that triggered.

Slide 4: Visibility: phase 1(about 55 s)

This is the visibility plot for phase one. Columns are LEDs, rows are detectors. The number is how often the detector triggers when that LED flashes. The red box is the LED's own detector, and it is almost always one. A number next to a red box means the neighbouring detector also sees the light.

Slide 5: Visibility: phase 2(about 35 s)

Phase two shows the same pattern. The own detector triggers almost every time, and often the next detector too. The light only reaches the next detector, never further.

Slide 6: Stability: amplitude vs time(about 50 s)

Second, amplitude versus time. Each row is one cell, each column is one series, in time order. White means normal. Most of it is white: 91 percent of the points are within 10 percent. So it is mostly stable, but a few spots need a second look.

Slide 7: Amplitude: cases to look at again(about 50 s)

These need a second look. The clearest one: early on September 18, Z20's LEDs became much dimmer, and a few hours later they were back to normal. Z19 also saw Z20's LED get dimmer, so it is the LED, not the detector. Also, on the first day a few cells are a bit high.

Slide 8: Stability: trigger fraction vs time(about 55 s)

Third, trigger fraction versus time. Yellow means it triggers on every flash. The own-LED cells stay yellow: stable. Other cells have steps. I checked the ODB: the steps are exactly where the trigger thresholds were changed, the black lines. So these are setting changes, not unstable detectors.

Slide 9: Template generation: method(about 65 s)

Fourth, templates. First, how they are made. Step one: select the events where this detector triggered on this LED's flash, and use all twelve channels. Step two: a very short median filter removes the narrow spike from the LED drive. Step three: a 100 kilohertz low-pass filter. Step four: subtract the baseline and divide by the peak. Step five: fit each pulse with two exponentials, with a free start time. The top right plot shows one pulse and its fit. Step six: shift each measured pulse so that the fitted start is at the same time. Step seven: average all aligned pulses of the 45 series. That is the template, the red line at the bottom right. The fit is only used to find the start time; the template itself is the mean of the measured pulses. Finally we fit the template with one rise and two falls to get the analytic pulse.

Slide 10: Templates: example(about 30 s)

Here is Z2 as an example, twelve channels. Blue are the aligned single pulses, red is the template, and the dashed black line is the analytic pulse. Channels with a grey title have no clear pulse, so we do not fit an analytic pulse there.

Slide 11: Example pulses: Z7 PSum with Z7's LED(about 60 s)

Prof. Liu wanted to see how the pulse shape changes with time. This is Z7 with its own LED, all twelve channels summed: the PSum. One pulse per series, 45 in total. Light to dark is early to late. On the left, raw ADC: the amplitude slowly goes down. The sharp negative spike at the start is pickup from the LED drive. On the right, each pulse is divided by its own peak, so we only see the shape. They lie on top of each other: the shape did not change.

Slide 12: Summary(about 40 s)

To sum up. Visibility: each LED is seen by its own detector, and often by the next one. Amplitude: mostly stable; Z20's LEDs on September 18 need a closer look. Trigger fraction: stable; the steps are threshold changes. Templates: one for every cell, and the Z7 pulse shape did not change during the week. Thank you.

Likely questions (one or two sentences each)

Q1. Why no detector further away?

Only the triggering detector is read out. If the light is below threshold, we cannot see it.

Q2. Was the Z20 drop the LED or the detector?

It looks like the LED. Z19 also saw Z20's LED get dimmer, but Z19 with its own LED was normal. We do not know why yet.

Q3. Who changed the trigger thresholds?

I do not know. I only see the changes in the ODB. We should ask the people on shift.

Q4. How exactly are the templates made?

For each pulse: median filter, 100 kilohertz low-pass, subtract the baseline, divide by the peak, fit two exponentials to find the start, and shift the measured pulse to that start. Then we average the pulses of all series. The fit is only used to find the start.

Q5. Some templates have a spike at the start?

Those are own-LED cells: the LED drive is picked up by the readout. The neighbouring detector sees the same LED cleanly, so we can use its template.
